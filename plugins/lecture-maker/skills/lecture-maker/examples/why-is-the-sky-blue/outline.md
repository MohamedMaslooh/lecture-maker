# Why is the sky blue?: outline

**Speaker:** (example) · **Audience:** curious adults, no physics background · **Length:** ~4 minutes
**Language:** slides in English, spoken in Egyptian Arabic · **The one thing to leave with:** the air
scatters short (blue) waves far more than long (red) ones; a longer path at sunset leaves the reds.

## Slide by slide

**1. Why is the sky blue?**
- **Topic:** none · **Kicker:** An example lecture · made with Lecture Maker
- **Visual:** the title over a drawn sky, noon blue at the top to a sunset band at the horizon.
- **On screen:** the title. **Source:** none. **Pictures:** none (drawn).

**2. What is sunlight made of?**
- **Topic:** Light · **Kicker:** Start with the light itself
- **Visual:** the sun, a white beam, a prism. *Click 1:* the colours fan out, named violet to red, and
  one line arrives.
- **On screen:** "White sunlight is all the colours of the rainbow together."
- **The point:** white light carries every colour; the prism only separates them.
- **Source:** NASA Space Place, "Why Is the Sky Blue?" (true). **Pictures:** none (drawn).

**3. Which colour does the air scatter most?**
- **Topic:** Scattering · **Kicker:** Air bounces light around
- **Visual:** words left with two waves drawing themselves (short blue, long red). *Click 1:* bars:
  blue (450 nm) ~5.9x, red (700 nm) 1x, tagged "our calculation" with (700/450)^4.
- **The point:** shorter waves scatter far more (Rayleigh, 1/wavelength^4); the 5.9 is our own
  arithmetic with two example wavelengths.
- **Source:** NASA Space Place (true) · Wikipedia, "Rayleigh scattering" (true). **Pictures:** none.

**4. Why are sunsets red?**
- **Topic:** Sunset · **Kicker:** Same sun, same air, longer road
- **Visual:** a real photo of a sunset over the sea fills the stage (blue sky overhead, orange at
  the horizon). *Click 1:* the photo shrinks to the left (a past step) and two drawn scenes fade up
  beside it: noon, the sun overhead and a short path; sunset, the sun at the horizon and a long path.
- **The point:** the longer the path through air, the more blue is scattered out on the way.
- **Source:** NASA Space Place (true) · Wikipedia, "Rayleigh scattering" (true). **Pictures:**
  `I01-sea-sunset.jpg`, Aleksandr Aleksandrov, "Sea Sunset", CC0 (Wikimedia Commons), credited in the source line.

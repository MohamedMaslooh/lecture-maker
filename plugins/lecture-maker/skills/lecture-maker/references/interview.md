# Day 1 · The brief and the questions

## The brief

Expect one long unstructured message, often dictated: ideas in the order they occurred, repeated
words, half-decisions ("maybe X, or rather Y, what do you think?"). This is good input. Speaking
gives you more context than typing would. Never ask the speaker to restructure it.

Pull out of it, silently:
- the subject, and the angle they actually care about
- the audience and what they already know
- anything they want to give the audience (a handout, a file, a tool)
- the decisions they already made, and the ones they left open ("what do you think?")
- whether sources matter a lot or little for this talk

## Read before you reply

Without being asked, read: the project instructions, `house-rules.md`, memory (if the platform has
it), the series plan, the last lecture's outline and trail, and any talk on the same subject the
audience already heard. Tell the speaker in one line what you read, so they know why one messy
message was enough.

## The first reply

1. One idea, with your recommendation. Not a menu of five options.
2. A rough shape: parts or chapters in the order they make sense, the opening and the close.
3. The questions, each with **your suggested answer** in the same line, so they can say yes or
   change one. Five to eight. Number them.
4. End by saying nothing will be built until they say go.

## The question bank

Pick only what is still open after reading. Write each as: **question** · suggested: ...

- Who exactly is in the room, and what do they already know? · suggested: from the series plan
- How long, which date, which room and screen? · suggested: the series' usual length
- The one thing the room should walk out with? · suggested: your reading of the brief
- How many sources does this talk need: every claim, or only the numbers and quotes? · suggested:
  only where a claim needs one
- Real examples from your own work, or general ones? May real conversations be shown (as
  paraphrase, never word for word)? · suggested: paraphrase, no names
- Any live moment (a demo, a question to the room)? · suggested: none for the first version
- Anything to hand out at the end (a file, a link, a QR)? · suggested: the deck itself
- Language of the slides and of the talk? · suggested: slides in English, the talk in the language
  they speak
- Pictures: real photos you have, or should I find real ones / generate where nothing real exists?
  · suggested: real first, I will list them for your OK
- Anything the audience must not see (patient details, names, internal documents)? · suggested:
  nothing private on any slide
- Which tools besides this one may I use (browser, image tools)? · suggested: only what is needed

## Answers that change the plan

When an answer moves something ("voice goes to the end", "drop the live demo"), restate the new
shape in two or three lines. When an answer is a correction of how you work, it becomes a rule
(see review-loop.md, "Corrections become rules").

If the speaker asks what a term in your reply means, explain it plainly, then continue. Do not take
a question as a go.

# Day 1 · The outline, agreed in chat

The outline is drafted and changed in the conversation, before any file or agent exists. Changing
a plan costs a minute; changing a built deck costs an hour.

## In the chat: the short form

One line per slide, numbered, grouped by part:

```
Part 1 · Light
 2. What is sunlight made of?  · prism; click 1: the colours fan out · source: NASA
 3. Which colour does the air scatter most?  · two waves; click 1: bars, blue ~5.9x red (our calculation) · source: NASA, Rayleigh
```

Then the time budget (minutes per part, total), and the open questions with suggested answers.
Mark any fact not yet checked `[CHECK]`.

## After the go: the long form in `outline.md`

Per slide (see `examples/why-is-the-sky-blue/outline.md`):
- **title** (plain: says what the slide explains) · **topic** (for the strip at the top) · **kicker**
- **visual** and what each click shows (one click per group; past steps shrink)
- **on screen**: the exact few words
- **the point**: what the slide is for, in one line
- **source**: one line, or "none"
- **pictures**: what is needed, real or generated, file name
- **who builds it** (when several builders work)

## Checks before saying "ready to build"

- every slide has one idea; no two slides say the same thing
- titles are plain; no clever headlines
- no two neighbouring slides share a layout (write the layout name per slide and read down the list)
- no analogies from the audience's own profession forced onto the subject
- the time budget adds up (at this density ~1 slide per minute)
- every number, quote and date has a source planned, or is cut
- the close gives the audience something to do or take

## The gate

Stop after the outline. Say what you will do on the go (which agents, which models, roughly how
long). Wait for "go", "start", "build it". "Looks good" is not a go; ask "Shall I start building?"
